NamEngine Social Media Launch Brief
Executive Summary
NamEngine is a personalized naming platform built to help people
discover names that genuinely fit their taste.
The core idea is simple: most naming tools give users more names.
NamEngine is designed to understand what a person means when
they say things like:
“Classic, but not boring.”
“Distinctive, but not strange.”
“Strong, but still warm.”
“Traditional, but not too common.”
“Irish influence, but easy to use in America.”
NamEngine turns those preferences into a taste profile, generates a
curated set of names, learns from the user’s reactions, and improves
its recommendations over successive rounds.
The platform currently supports three naming verticals:
Baby names
Pet names
Business names
Baby is the lead consumer experience and should be the primary
focus of the initial social launch.
NamEngine is not positioned as “just another AI name generator.”
Its long-term differentiation is that it is becoming a true taste engine:
a system that learns what someone likes, why they like it, what they
reject, where their preferences conflict, and how to make
increasingly better recommendations over time.
The social media strategy should reflect that distinction.
The brand should become known not merely for providing lists of
names, but for understanding names: their sound, history, meaning,

1. Social Media Objectives
The first 30 days should accomplish five things.
Build brand awareness
Introduce NamEngine to prospective users without overwhelming
them with product promotion.
Establish naming authority
Create content that demonstrates intelligence about names,
including sound, history, popularity, meaning, culture, and style.
Generate participation
Names are more interesting than you think.
“This account understands names.”
“This product might actually understand my taste.”

Names naturally create opinions. Polls, comparisons, debates, and
preference questions should encourage comments, votes, shares,
and saves.
Introduce the concept of taste
Gradually teach the audience that choosing names is not simply
about finding names people like. It is about identifying patterns in
what they like.
Drive qualified traffic
By the second half of the launch period, begin directing interested
users toward the NamEngine experience.

5. Core Content Franchises

A successful social strategy should rely on repeatable content
formats rather than inventing an entirely new concept every day.
Name DNA
Explain why a particular name feels the way it does.
Examples:
Why Margot feels vintage and modern at the same time
Why James never seems to go out of style
Why certain names sound strong
Why some names feel familiar without feeling common
Purpose:
Establish NamEngine as an authority on naming and reinforce the
concept that taste has identifiable characteristics.
If You Love…
Take a familiar name and recommend alternatives based on what
someone might actually love about it.
Example:
If you love Theodore but it feels too popular…
Possible alternatives might include names that share:
historical depth
rhythm
formality
nickname potential
traditional feel
This is particularly powerful because it mirrors what the NamEngine
product itself does.
Would You Name Them?
Simple head-to-head or multi-name choices.

Examples:
Arthur vs. August
Maeve vs. Margot
Felix vs. Finn
Clara vs. Elodie
Purpose:
Generate participation while collecting interesting aggregate
preference signals.
Nobody's Talking About…
Feature excellent underused names.
Each piece should briefly explain:
the name
origin or history
meaning where relevant
pronunciation where needed
what makes it interesting
why NamEngine thinks it deserves consideration
Purpose:
Make NamEngine a source of discovery.
The Naming Debate
Questions that naturally produce disagreement without having a
single correct answer.
Examples:
Would you avoid a name because it is becoming popular?
Would you use a name your sibling already used?
Would you rather correct pronunciation or share your name with
three classmates?
Does an unusual spelling make a name more distinctive or
more difficult?

Would popularity stop you from using the name you love?
Purpose:
Comments, community, and insight into how people make naming
decisions.
Taste Test
Show several names representing different style territories.
Example:
A. Clara
B. Margot
C. Maeve
D. Elodie
Purpose:
Introduce NamEngine's concept of taste without needing to describe
the underlying technology.

9. Social Media as a Taste Laboratory
Social media can eventually provide NamEngine with valuable
aggregate insight into naming preferences.
Examples:
Arthur vs. August
Familiar vs. distinctive
Strong vs. soft
Traditional vs. contemporary
Common spelling vs. alternative spelling
This should initially be treated as research rather than automatically
influencing the NamEngine production recommendation engine.
Over time, anonymized aggregate patterns could become a
proprietary dataset.
Potential future branded research product:
The NamEngine Taste Index
Possible reports:
Names people love but say they would not use
Most polarizing names
Rising naming styles
Most appealing underused names
Generational differences in taste
Familiarity versus distinctiveness preferences
This could eventually support media coverage, PR, and original
NamEngine research.
